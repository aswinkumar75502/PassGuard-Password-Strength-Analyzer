Create passwords
attackers hate

Practical, proven strategies backed by NIST, OWASP, and leading cybersecurity researchers. Apply these principles and your accounts become dramatically harder to compromise.

📏

Length is King

Every additional character multiplies the search space exponentially for a brute-force attacker.

→Aim for 16+ characters as your baseline
→A 20-character passphrase is stronger than an 8-char complex password
→Never go below 12 characters for any account that matters
🎲

Use a Passphrase

Passphrases combine multiple random words into something long but memorable.

→correct-horse-battery-staple is far stronger than P@ss1!
→Use 4–6 completely random words (avoid personal words)
→Sprinkle numbers and symbols between words for extra entropy
🔁

Never Reuse Passwords

Credential stuffing attacks test billions of leaked credentials against other services automatically.

→Every account must have a unique password — no exceptions
→Use a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, KeePassXC) to remember them all
→Check haveibeenpwned.com regularly to see if your email was in a breach
🧩

Mix Character Classes

Using all four character classes forces attackers to search a vastly larger space.

→Uppercase: A–Z (26 options per character)
→Lowercase: a–z (26 options per character)
→Numbers: 0–9 (10 options)
→Special characters: ~30 symbols on a standard keyboard
🚫

What to Avoid

These patterns are the first things dictionary and rule-based attacks try:

✗Personal info: names, birthdays, pet names, phone numbers
✗Common substitutions: p@ssw0rd, S3cur!ty
✗Keyboard patterns: qwerty, 123456, asdfgh
✗Dictionary words on their own, even in other languages
✗Repeated characters: aaaa, 1111
🔐

Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Even a perfect password can be phished or leaked. 2FA adds a second barrier.

→Use an authenticator app (Authy, Google Authenticator) over SMS when possible
→Hardware keys (YubiKey) offer the strongest protection against phishing
→Enable 2FA on email, banking, and social accounts first — they're highest value
🗄️

Use a Password Manager

The only practical way to have unique, complex passwords for every site.

→Bitwarden – open source, free tier, audited, cross-platform
→1Password – polished UI, family sharing, Travel Mode
→KeePassXC – fully offline, maximum control, zero cloud dependency
→Your master password for the manager should be your strongest password ever
🔄

When to Change Your Password

Modern NIST guidance no longer recommends arbitrary periodic changes — they cause weaker choices.

→Change immediately if you suspect compromise or receive a breach notification
→Change after a service you use announces a data breach
→Do NOT change on a fixed schedule if the password is strong and unique
